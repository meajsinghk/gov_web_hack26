Frequently Asked Questions & Procedures

Authoritative guidance on 1930 emergency liens, evidence requirements, anonymous complaints, and judicial refunds.

Q1: What is the "Golden Hour" and what happens when I report financial fraud?

The Golden Hour refers to the initial 2 to 4 hours immediately following an unauthorized transaction. Reporting within this window via the 60-Second Express form or helpline 1930 triggers the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting & Management System (CFCFRMS) to push real-time API lien alerts across 250+ connected banks and payment gateways, placing a temporary hold on the suspect mule account before funds are cashed out at ATMs.

Q2: Where do I find the mandatory 12-digit Transaction UTR / RRN number?

The Unique Transaction Reference (UTR) or Retrieval Reference Number (RRN) is a 12-digit numeric identifier found on:

  • Your UPI app payment receipt (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM).
  • The official debit SMS alert sent by your bank.
  • Your NetBanking statement or bank passbook entry.

Q3: Can I report cyber crimes against women and children anonymously?

Yes. For severe offenses involving Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) or non-consensual sexually explicit imagery, you can file without providing your Aadhaar or identity proof. The report is routed directly to social media intermediaries and ISPs for emergency content removal under Section 69A of the Information Technology Act.

Q4: How does the Public Suspect Identifier Search work?

The Suspect Search engine cross-references queries against the I4C national registry of flagged mule VPAs, fraudulent phone numbers, bank accounts, and malicious APK domains reported across Indian police stations. It displays fraud severity risk scores (0–100) and case frequencies to alert citizens before transactions are finalized.

Q5: How do I get my money back once an interim lien has been placed on a mule account?

Once the bank confirms frozen funds on the beneficiary account, the investigating Cyber Police officer submits an interim report to the jurisdictional Magistrate Court. Under Section 457 of the CrPC / Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), the court issues a release order directing the holding bank to credit the frozen amount back to your original bank account.

Q6: What is a "Digital Arrest" and what should I do if I receive such a video call?

"Digital Arrest" is a complete fraud. Indian police, CBI, Customs, ED, or court judges never conduct arrests over Skype or WhatsApp video calls, nor do they ask citizens to stay in front of webcams or transfer money to "security escrow accounts". Immediately hang up and report the caller's number to 1930.

Q7: How can citizens join the National Cyber Crime Volunteer Program?

Any Indian citizen above 18 years of age can apply as a Cyber Volunteer on our Cyber Volunteers Portal to help flag unlawful online content or conduct local digital hygiene awareness workshops in their schools, colleges, and neighborhoods.

Q8: What is Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act and why is it needed?

Section 65B (and corresponding provisions under Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam) provides statutory admissibility for electronic records such as chat exports, transaction screenshots, and server logs. When you download our digitally stamped Complaint PDF, forensic hash metadata is applied to support legal validity during trial.
